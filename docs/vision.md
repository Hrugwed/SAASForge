# SaaSForge product definition

## Product

SaaSForge is a public, AI-native SaaS factory. A developer clones it, configures trusted providers, generates an isolated product, implements only the product-specific experience and business value, verifies it, and deploys it through a documented path.

It is not a finished SaaS, a visual theme, a marketplace of arbitrary integrations, or a promise that unique product logic requires no engineering.

## Users

The primary users are solo technical founders and small product teams who value secure defaults, transparent ownership, fast iteration, and the ability to replace providers without rewriting product logic.

## Product promise

A clean clone must make commodity SaaS work repeatable:

- Local environment setup is diagnosed rather than guessed.
- Products are generated from validated configuration.
- Authentication, organizations, billing, email, analytics, observability, jobs, storage, admin, privacy, and security are selectable capabilities.
- Products remain independently deployable and independently recoverable.
- Codex and Claude can navigate and modify the repository through concise instructions, scoped documentation, reusable skills, and executable verification.
- Production readiness is evidence-based and cannot be claimed by a dashboard checkbox alone.

## Success measures

- A prepared developer reaches a running generated product in under 30 minutes.
- A new product can be generated in under five minutes without manual file copying.
- The agent can identify the correct package and verification workflow from the root guide without scanning unrelated applications.
- No generated product shares production data, secrets, billing state, or deployment failure domains with another product by default.
- Two disposable validation products with different module profiles pass the complete readiness suite.
- Backup restoration and rollback are exercised, not merely documented.

## Architecture principles

1. Secure defaults and fail-closed behavior.
2. Modular monolith before distributed systems.
3. Stable capability contracts around replaceable providers.
4. Independent products over a shared production blast radius.
5. Deterministic automation over repeated prompting.
6. Progressive context disclosure over giant instruction files.
7. Recorded evidence over claims.
